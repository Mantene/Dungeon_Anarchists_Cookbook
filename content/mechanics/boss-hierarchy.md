# Boss Hierarchy

Every dungeon floor contains a structured hierarchy of boss encounters. Killing a boss yields loot boxes, persistent drops, and leaderboard stars. The tier system scales from neighborhood-level threats to the single Floor Boss that ends the floor.

---

## Tiers

| Tier | Loot Box | Persistent Drop | Notes |
|------|----------|-----------------|-------|
| **Neighborhood** | Bronze | Neighborhood map (~1 sq mile) | Most common; found throughout the floor |
| **Borough** | Silver | Borough field guide (mob types in nearby quadrants) | Multiple per floor |
| **City** | Gold | — | One per major city on the floor |
| **Province** | Platinum | — | Regional boss; rare |
| **Country** | Legendary | — | One per floor; often a major plot encounter |
| **Floor** | Celestial | — | Single floor boss; killing it opens or accelerates stairwells |

**Persistent loot** (maps, field guides) drops for **any crawler** who finds the corpse — not just the killing party. The killing party additionally receives the boss box and a ranking star appended to their leaderboard name.

---

## Key Rules

- **Bosses can leave their rooms** on Floors 1–3 (established when the Krakaren Clone exited her chamber on Floor 2)
- **Elites** are not part of the tier hierarchy — they are producer-written NPCs with "plot armor" in Act 1 of their storyline. Do not murder-hobo an elite in their first scene. Examples: Tsarina Signet (Floor 3), Grimaldi's Circus (Floor 3)
- **Player-character bosses**: Dungeon-born NPCs can be assigned boss tiers — Ferdinand the cat was a Level 100 Province Boss (Book 5) as Queen Imogen's familiar

## Loot Box Tiers (Full Scale)

Bronze → Silver → Gold → Platinum → Legendary → **Celestial**

Special box types that do not follow the tier hierarchy: Benefactor (patron-sent, most valuable), Adventurer, Weapon, Pet, Apparel, Asshole (morally questionable achievements).
