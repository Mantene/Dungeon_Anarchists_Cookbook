# Book 1 Chapter 28: Studio Interview

## Summary
[[carl]] and [[princess-donut]] wait in the green room of the production trailer. [[lexis]] explains that they are in a physical trailer on the Earth's surface, but the studio set is a virtual holodeck; the audience and host are projected from off-world. Donut takes charge of the media strategy, asking Lexis about the show's tone, editing, and audience. Carl looks out a porthole at the ocean but realizes there is no escape. 

They walk onto the bright stage. The audience consists of a massive, shadowy sea of diverse alien races. The host, **[[odette]]**, is a crab-taur: she has a bear-sized king crab lower body, a praying mantis head with compound eyes and long antennae, and a naked human female torso with absurdly enlarged breasts. 

Donut immediately charms the audience, explaining her "GC" (Grand Champion) title and claiming that Earth cats have an "elective monarchy." Odette prompts Carl to say his "catchphrase." They play a compilation of clips showing Carl yelling *"Goddamnit, Donut!"* in various near-death situations. Carl obliges, saying the phrase to wild applause. 

They show a clip of their fight with the Hoarder, which makes Carl deeply uncomfortable as he confronts his own terror on screen. Donut promises the audience that they won't just kill monsters—they will *"kill them big... with style."* As the segment ends, Carl realizes that Donut has become instantly addicted to the crowd's adoring approval, which represents a dangerous new threat.

## Themes & Plot Threads
- **[[televised-exploitation]]**: The talk show interview showcases how the crawlers' genuine trauma and fear are packaged, edited, and turned into viral catchphrases and entertainment for a galactic audience.

## Personal Interpretation & Reflections
Donut's transition into a media superstar is swift. Her show-cat training makes her perfectly suited to manipulate the audience, but Carl sees the danger: she is starting to view the crawl as a game of style and approval rather than a desperate struggle for survival. Carl's catchphrase, "Goddamnit, Donut!", is officially minted and commodified, illustrating how the system co-opts the crawlers' authentic desperation.
