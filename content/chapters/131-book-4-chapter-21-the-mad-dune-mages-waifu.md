# Book 4 Chapter 21: The Mad Dune Mage's Waifu

## Summary
Carl's team explores the glass castle, confronts the Mad Dune Mage Ghazi, and learns the bizarre truth behind his transmutation and the sand ooze.
- **Glass Castle Exploration**: Everything in the castle has been transmuted into glass (*fulgurite*) and frozen in time. In a fountain, they find a glass statue of a half-naiad, half-human female holding her arms out, with a plaque reading "Lika, my love." Mongo accidentally knocks it over, shattering it. Carl loots its sticky glass head.
- **Peephole Reconnaissance**: In the basement, they find a normal wooden door leading to the boss chamber. Donut uses *Astral Paw* to knock over magazines to draw out the boss. The occupant is **Ghazi** (Level 43 Glass Mage), wearing a bathrobe.
- **The Backstory**: Ghazi is a "neckbeard" mage who came to the Necropolis to summon Yarilo (god of Lust) to turn his fictional "waifu" Lika (portrayed by a Lika love doll) into a real woman. Instead of Yarilo, he was tricked by **Psamathe** (a lesser deity of the Nothing).
- **The Curse of the Sand Ooze**: After Ghazi "consummated" the marriage in the dark, he realized the deity had given him a "wife of sand"—a massive **Sand Ooze** (the familiar of Psamathe). During the daily sandstorm, Ghazi returns to human form while his wife turns into a slime. The rest of the time, the ooze assumes Lika's form while Ghazi is turned to sand (and the ooze "eats" him daily to express love).
- **The Standoff**: Carl triggers his *Protective Shell* to push back the encroaching sand, which is actually the hostile Sand Ooze. Carl punches Ghazi, steals his leather bag (containing a *Letter from the Council* and the *Mysterious Winding Box*), and they retreat up the stairs as the ooze slowly pursues them.
- **The Letter's Revelation**: The letter from the Tome of Scholars reveals that Psamathe's soul was split—half is in the Lika doll, and half is in the ghost of Quetzalcoatlus. If the changelings succeed in resurrecting Quetzalcoatlus, Psamathe will be fully restored, forcing the "true gods" to react. A postscript from "Tish" curses Ghazi for abandoning her.

## Personal Interpretation & Reflections
The revelation that the "Lika" statue was a cleaned-out sex doll that Ghazi tried to bring to life is incredibly dark and funny. The mechanics of the sand ooze's "love" (eating Ghazi while he is turned to sand) shows the deeply twisted nature of the entities summoned from the Nothing.
