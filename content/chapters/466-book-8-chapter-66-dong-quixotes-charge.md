# Chapter 466: Dong Quixote's Charge

## Summary
The deity [[grull]] appears, granting all four-legged creatures temporary double power to their attacks. [[carl]]'s truck glides through the air using the Bubble Buddy shield after driving off a balcony into a crate-filled room. 

[[grull]] crashes through the wall, spilling molten metal and targeting Carl. [[princess-donut]] casts *Laundry Day*, ejecting the controller—[[the-maestro]]—from the god's chest. The Maestro falls into the molten metal (he does not die permanently, but is sent to his quarters on the 12th floor). Without his controller, Grull acts on divine instinct, targeting [[corcunda]] for death.

With the exit blocked by a pileup, Corcunda decides he must face Grull to save the group. [[dong-quixote]], whose health is almost entirely depleted, refuses to leave Corcunda's side. Dong pulls out his massive lance, and Donut casts *Big-Ass Magnet* on it. Dong rides astride Corcunda, charging Grull. The magnet pulls millions of spilled screws through the air, shredding the Slag Elementals and collapsing them. Dong lowers his lance and strikes Grull. Grull rumbles "A good death indeed" before smiting both Dong and Corcunda, then vanishes. 

Carl drives the damaged truck through the exit, crossing the finish line with 15 seconds left. The quest **"I have dreamed thee too long"** is complete.

## Characters
- [[carl]] — Present, drives the truck and navigates the crash.
- [[princess-donut]] — Present, casts *Laundry Day* and *Big-Ass Magnet*.
- [[dong-quixote]] — Present, charges Grull astride Corcunda with his lance, dies a heroic death.
- [[corcunda]] — Present, reunited and smited by Grull.
- [[grull]] — Present, the deity of war who smites Dong and Corcunda.
- [[the-maestro]] — Present, ejected from Grull's chest by Laundry Day.
- [[grigori]] — Present, injured in the crash but survives.

## Themes & Plot Threads
- **"I have dreamed thee too long"**: Quest completed with the tragic, heroic death of Dong Quixote and Corcunda.
- **A Good Death**: Dong Quixote fulfilling his dream of tilting a giant alongside his love.

## Personal Interpretation & Reflections
Dong Quixote's charge is one of the most cinematic and emotionally resonant moments in the series. The combination of the "Big-Ass Magnet" spell pulling millions of screws to shred the elementals while Dong rides a mantaur into battle against a god of war is a perfect blend of high-concept fantasy action and emotional payoff.
