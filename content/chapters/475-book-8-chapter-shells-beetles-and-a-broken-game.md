# Chapter 475: Chapter 75 — Shells, Beetles, and a Broken Game

## Summary
Carl, Princess Donut, and Nester continue their descent through the apartment building on Floor 10, encountering various simulated "shells" of people and animals. On the fifth floor, they briefly see Annabeth in a bathroom before a [[find-traps]] skill reveals a freeze trap. The gate is a kitchen sink drain.

They proceed to the fourth floor, an apartment where a woman named Mackenzie is screaming, searching for her son Ollie. Photos reveal her to be the wife of [[archie-mu]], the ladybug guy from the Pineapple Cabaret. Mackenzie's eyes are X'd out in photos, but Ollie's are not, suggesting the simulation believes him to be alive. The gate is hidden in Ollie's cluttered bedroom. As they leave, Mackenzie begins to tear at her stitched eyes in horror.

Carl receives a message from [[louis-santiago-2]], who reports that "beetles" are pouring out of a collapsed person on the sixth floor, causing chaos.

They drop to the third floor, landing in apartment 310, which is filled with people, all with X'd-out eyes, surrounding a man having a seizure. Carl realizes this is a simulation of [[imani-c]]'s family, including her father (the seizing man) and mother. Imani appears, haggard, and picks up a Maltese dog named Gucci, whose eyes are notably not X'd out. Carl warns [[elle-mcgibbons]] about the disturbing scene.

As Carl's team navigates towards the window gate, [[deontay]], one of the simulated family members, throws a PlayStation controller at them. The simulated Imani's mother begins to rip at her own face, and then transforms into a "Ruptured Feast Hive," with beetles pouring out of her face, eyes, and mouth. The other simulated family members also freeze, becoming hives from which beetles emerge.

Upon clearing the fifth gate, the System reveals that the "people, doggies, cats, fish, that turtle...the monkey" encountered in the apartments are "shells" living in loops. The intention was for racers to break these loops, causing the shells to release "prizes" (bugs) that would then coalesce on the roof into a gate boss called the "Sacred Guardian of the Immortal Tem." However, due to "death-cult bullshit" and a god's interference (implied to be [[khepri]]), the game has an "error." The invulnerable Sacred Feaster Scarab Beetles, originally meant to ignore the racers, are now aggressive and will actively try to kill them, maintaining their invulnerability. A wave of these large, flying beetles swarms after Carl's vehicle.

## Themes & Plot Threads
- [[systemic-oppression]] — The dungeon's design for entertainment, turning simulated lives into disposable "shells" for game mechanics, highlights the profound cruelty of the System.
- [[survival]] — The constant immediate threat of disintegration, navigating traps, and now, aggressive, invulnerable monsters.
- [[televised-exploitation]] — The System's detailed explanation of the "shells" and "prizes" confirms the horrific nature of the dungeon as a spectacle.
- [[dungeon-mechanics]] — The revelation of "shells" and their intended purpose, as well as the catastrophic "game error" that changes their function from passive to aggressive.
- [[simulated-realities]] — The apartments function as distorted simulations of past lives, and the psychological horror of seeing these simulations break down.
- [[divine-interference]] — The System explicitly blames a god (implied to be [[khepri]]) for causing the "death-cult bullshit" error that has disrupted the floor's intended mechanics.
- [[moral-dilemmas]] — Carl and Donut are forced to witness the suffering and grotesque transformations of these simulated beings, struggling with the emotional impact.

## Personal Interpretation & Reflections
This chapter takes the already morally ambiguous Floor 10 to a new level of horror and systemic cruelty. The reveal that the apartment inhabitants are "shells" designed to break open and release monsters is a chilling insight into the dungeon's true nature, solidifying the idea that the universe views sentient life as mere components for its grotesque entertainment. The breaking of Mackenzie's and Imani's simulated family members is deeply unsettling, a visceral depiction of the simulations' fragility and the suffering they endure.

The System's "confession" about the game being broken due to "death-cult bullshit" and a god's interference is a significant plot development. It not only raises the stakes by introducing aggressive, invulnerable monsters but also hints at larger cosmic conflicts impacting the dungeon itself. The System's crude, yet darkly humorous, analogy about "flaccid wangs" and "perfect tootsies" underscores its chaotic, amoral nature even as it explains a severe, potentially fatal, bug in its own design. This chapter effectively combines psychological horror with a major shake-up of the dungeon's rules, leaving the protagonists in an even more precarious situation.
