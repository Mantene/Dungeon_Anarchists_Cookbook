# Book 3 Chapter 26: Harvesting Hands and Growler Gary

## Summary
At Station 75, Carl, Donut, and Katia enter the Downward Dog tavern and find the sole survivor, a Level 25 Shade Gnoll bartender named **Growler Gary**. Gary is heavily drunk and rambling about the attack on the station. He explains that an invisible boundary wall prevented him from leaving the tavern to fight alongside his friend, Jumping Jen-Jen, who called him a coward before dying. Gary also mentions a woman (likely Maggie My) who ripped his throat out and stole his alcohol, though he resurrected shortly after.

Gary informs Carl about the **Interdiction Rapid-Response Railway Repair Carts**, battery-powered vehicles equipped with mobile clean-up portals. These portals can sweep the tracks and teleport debris (or trains/passengers) to either the trainyards or the abyss. However, starting these carts requires a security clearance: two gnoll hands must be placed on sensor plates located on opposite sides of the cockpit.

To obtain the biometric keys, Carl and Donut realize they must harvest Gary's hands. They allow Mongo to kill the sleeping Gary to gain experience. However, when Carl tests the first cart, he discovers it shuts off immediately if a hand is removed from the console. 

Returning to the bar, they find Gary resurrected and sober. He is terrified and brandishes a spear to defend himself. Carl stops the attack and explains the rescue plan: they need to clear three colored lines to save the crawlers trapped at the abyss, which requires sending two carts down each track (one to sweep ghouls into the abyss, and a second to sweep crawlers to the trainyards). Gary, understanding the goal and promised alcohol, cracks his neck and willingly lets them kill him fourteen times to harvest enough hands.

- **[[survival-of-the-collapse]]**: The plan coordinates with Bautista's groups who are hiding near different colored lines.
- **[[systemic-oppression]]**: The exploitation of quest-essential NPC resurrection mechanics for biometric harvesting highlights the brutal compromises forced by the dungeon's design.

## Personal Interpretation & Reflections
The scene where Growler Gary willingly submits to being killed repeatedly is one of the most haunting and tragic moments of the crawl. It showcases the dark, utilitarian logic that Carl is forced to adopt to save human lives. Gary's willingness to endure fourteen agonizing deaths out of loyalty to his deceased friends and a desire to help the crawlers highlights the genuine humanity of the NPCs, contrasting sharply with the Syndicate's view of them as disposable, programmed tools.
