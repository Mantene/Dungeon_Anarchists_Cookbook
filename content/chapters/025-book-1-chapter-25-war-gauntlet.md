# Book 1 Chapter 25: War Gauntlet

## Summary
[[carl]] loots the gym's weights, benches, a laptop, files, and a bottle of Johnny Walker Black. They both reach Level 9 and head to the nearest training guild. [[mordecai]] reviews their fight notes and provides crucial feedback: Carl's bare-knuckle skills do just as much damage as unenchanted weapons. He also explains that scroll duration scales with Intelligence; if Donut (Int 24) had read the *Confusing Fog* scroll instead of Carl (Int 3), it would have lasted 120 seconds instead of 15. 

Donut reads a spellbook and learns **Puddle Jumper**, a short-range teleport spell. Mordecai tells them that entering a Borough Boss fight with only six crawlers is madness. He advises them to search for boss vulnerabilities (like the Juicer's neck vein). 

Carl opens his loot boxes:
- **Enchanted War Gauntlet of the Exalted Grull (Right-Handed)**: A wrist bracer that transforms into a spiked steel gauntlet when clenching a fist. It adds +3 Strength, +1 Dexterity, +2 Iron Punch, and +1 Powerful Strike. (Warning: 1.5% chance to transform equestrian targets into the god Grull).
- **Enchanted Pedicure Kit of the Sylph**: A kit that requires Carl to remain barefoot, providing +15% barefoot damage, +3 to Smush, the "Unbreakable" buff for his feet, and a 5-second trap trigger delay alarm.

Mordecai explains that Grull is an artificial deity and hints that bosses on lower floors are not just game constructs but sometimes "those who pay for the privilege" of playing as monsters.

## Themes & Plot Threads
- **[[carls-class-selection]]**: Carl's loot (the War Gauntlet and the Pedicure Kit) cements his combat specialization as an unarmed, barefoot brawler. Donut expands her magical repertoire with *Puddle Jumper*.
- **[[systemic-oppression]]**: Mordecai's cryptic comments about the artificiality of the dungeon's gods and the true nature of lower-floor bosses reveal that the crawl is a corporate sandbox where wealthy outsiders can buy their way in to play as monsters.

## Personal Interpretation & Reflections
The mechanics of the dungeon become clearer in this chapter. The system tailors loot to Carl's barefoot/unarmed playstyle (the pedicure kit and gauntlet), gamifying his choice to enter the dungeon without shoes. Mordecai's warning about the "gods" and "those who pay for the privilege" hints at the broader cosmic politics of the Syndicate.
